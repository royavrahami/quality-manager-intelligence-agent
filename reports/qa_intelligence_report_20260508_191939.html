AutoRISE: Agent-Driven Strategy Evolution for Red-Teaming Large Language Models
🕵️ AI Agents 23 Apr 2026 19:37 UTC
Summary
The paper introduces AutoRISE, a novel method for evolving attack strategies against large language models (LLMs) by optimizing executable attack programs instead of static prompts. This approach allows for dynamic structural changes in attack strategies, improving the success rate significantly across various models and datasets. AutoRISE operates without the need for fine-tuning or human intervention, leveraging a coding agent to iteratively refine strategies based on performance diagnostics.
Key Insights
▸ AutoRISE enhances attack strategies by allowing structural changes and control-flow modifications, unlike traditional prompt-level methods.
▸ The method significantly improves attack success rates, demonstrating a 17-point increase over the strongest baseline across various models.
▸ AutoRISE functions in a black-box setting, requiring no additional resources like GPU compute or human annotations, making it highly efficient and scalable.
💼 Why This Matters for Quality Managers

MemSearch-o1: Empowering Large Language Models with Reasoning-Aligned Memory Growth in Agentic Search
🕵️ AI Agents 19 Apr 2026 05:35 UTC
Summary
The paper introduces MemSearch-o1, a framework designed to enhance large language models (LLMs) by addressing the memory dilution problem in agentic search tasks. MemSearch-o1 improves memory management by dynamically growing and refining memory fragments, enabling more effective reasoning and retrieval of external knowledge. This approach shifts from traditional memory concatenation to a structured, path-based reasoning model, demonstrating improved performance across multiple benchmark datasets.
Key Insights
▸ MemSearch-o1 addresses the memory dilution problem by using reasoning-aligned memory growth and retracing.
▸ The framework enhances memory management by dynamically refining memory fragments and organizing them into a globally connected memory path.
▸ Experiments show that MemSearch-o1 improves the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, providing a foundation for memory-aware agentic intelligence.

From Reasoning to Agentic: Credit Assignment in Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models
🕵️ AI Agents 10 Apr 2026 16:17 UTC
Summary
The paper discusses the challenges of credit assignment in reinforcement learning for large language models, focusing on the transition from reasoning to agentic RL. It highlights the complexity of determining which actions in a long sequence led to a specific outcome, especially in agentic RL where interactions are multi-turn and environments are partially observable. The authors survey 47 credit assignment methods and propose a taxonomy based on assignment granularity and methodology. They also provide resources such as a machine-readable inventory, a reporting checklist, and a benchmark protocol specification to aid future research.
Key Insights
▸ Credit assignment in RL for LLMs requires new methods due to the complexity of agentic environments.
▸ A taxonomy of 47 CA methods helps categorize approaches by granularity and methodology, aiding in method selection.
▸ Resources like a reporting checklist and benchmark protocol can standardize and improve future CA research.
